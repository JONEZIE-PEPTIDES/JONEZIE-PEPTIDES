Reference Reconstitution Reference

Understand concentration before beginning laboratory preparation.

Concentration calculations begin with the amount of compound listed on the vial and the volume of diluent used. Accurate labels and consistent records help prevent confusion when multiple products or strengths are being studied.

Before You Begin

  • Confirm the product name and amount shown on the vial.
  • Determine the intended final laboratory concentration.
  • Verify the required dilution volume before opening the vial.
  • Keep single-compound products separate from multi-compound blends.
  • Record the product, lot, dilution volume, and preparation date together.

Keep Laboratory Records Consistent

  • Use the same concentration format throughout your records.
  • Label each prepared vial clearly.
  • Add diluent slowly along the inside wall of the vial.
  • Follow the laboratory's approved procedures and applicable product documentation.

Storage Storage & Handling

Protect research materials with consistent storage practices.

Appropriate temperature control, protection from light, and accurate documentation help preserve the integrity of research materials.

General Storage Considerations

  • Keep unopened lyophilized products cool, dry, and protected from direct light.
  • Review product-specific documentation before selecting a storage temperature.
  • After laboratory preparation, refrigerate when directed by the applicable documentation.
  • Minimize unnecessary temperature changes.
  • Record receipt, opening, preparation, and storage dates.

Organized Handling

  • Keep the product label, lot information, preparation record, and storage dates together.
  • Use consistent labels and documentation across all research materials.
  • Review product-specific guidance because requirements may vary by compound.
  • Follow your laboratory's standard operating procedures.

Labels Reading Product Labels

Confirm the product, strength, and vial format.

A careful label review helps distinguish between products, strengths, and multi-compound blends before laboratory work begins.

What to Confirm

  • The complete product or compound name.
  • The amount listed on the vial.
  • Whether the product contains one compound or a blend.
  • The vial or product format.
  • Any lot or batch information provided.
  • The corresponding COA or product documentation.

Review the Complete Listing

  • Match the vial label with the product page and order information.
  • Do not rely on color or packaging alone to identify a product.
  • Confirm every component listed on a blended product.
  • Contact Jonezie Labs if a label or document does not match the ordered product.

Quality COA Basics

Review product identity and testing information.

A Certificate of Analysis provides testing information associated with a product or lot. Always compare the document with the exact product being reviewed.

What to Review

  • Product or compound name.
  • Listed strength or sample identity.
  • Lot or batch number, when available.
  • Test date and testing laboratory.
  • Analytical method.
  • Reported purity or other tested characteristics.
  • Any notes, limitations, or qualifying information.

Match the Document Carefully

  • Open the relevant product page and locate its available documentation.
  • Confirm that the product and lot information correspond with the material being reviewed.
  • Read the complete report rather than relying on a single result.
  • Contact Jonezie Labs with questions about locating or matching a COA.

A COA provides analytical information for the tested sample. It does not establish suitability for human or veterinary use.

Storage Cold Chain Basics

Maintain stable storage conditions and accurate records.

Temperature control and consistent documentation are important when storing and handling temperature-sensitive research materials.

General Cold-Chain Considerations

  • Review product-specific storage information upon receipt.
  • Keep products protected from direct light.
  • Avoid unnecessary temperature changes.
  • Refrigerate prepared material when required by the applicable documentation.
  • Clearly identify every vial before placing it into storage.

Information to Document

  • Product name and strength.
  • Lot or batch number.
  • Date received.
  • Date opened or prepared.
  • Dilution volume, when applicable.
  • Storage location and temperature range.

Storage requirements can vary by product. Always review the applicable product documentation and your laboratory's standard operating procedures.

Vials Lyophilized Vial Basics

Inspect and handle lyophilized research products carefully.

Lyophilization produces dry material that may appear as a compact cake, powder, or loose material inside the vial. Appearance can vary and should be evaluated together with the label, product documentation, and condition of the vial.

Before Handling

  • Confirm the product name, listed amount, and lot information.
  • Inspect the vial for cracks, damaged seals, or other visible damage.
  • Review the product documentation and laboratory procedures.
  • Record the product's condition before preparation.

General Handling Considerations

  • Introduce diluent slowly along the inside wall of the vial.
  • Avoid aggressive shaking.
  • Allow the material time to dissolve using gentle handling.
  • Label the vial with the preparation date and concentration.
  • Store it according to the applicable documentation.

This information is provided as a general laboratory reference and does not replace product-specific documentation or laboratory standard operating procedures.
